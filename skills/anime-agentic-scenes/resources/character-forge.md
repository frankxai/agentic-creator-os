# Character forge — designing cast that wins

A character in this series is not a person wearing the metaphor. The character **is** the mechanism, made into a body someone would cosplay. The bar is the top of competitive character design: readable at 64 pixels, unmistakable in silhouette, one signature asset, a colour identity nobody else in the cast shares, a history written on the gear, and a story the viewer can guess before a word is spoken.

The first Ren turnaround failed every one of these. Lean man, long coat, dark hair, gloves. That is the default the model returns when no design was done. This document is the design.

---

## The seven tests (a design fails if any one fails)

| Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | Filled black, 64 px tall, the character is identified among the whole cast. No two cast silhouettes share a shape family. |
| **Three-value read** | Squinted to dark / mid / light, the signature asset is the light. |
| **Signature asset** | One object or body feature that only this character has and that carries their mechanism. Remove it and the character stops being them. |
| **Colour identity** | One dominant, one accent. Unique pair in the cast. The cast palette is a lock: no two characters may share a dominant. |
| **Wear history** | Five marks of history on the design (repairs, burns, worn edges, a personal object, an inherited piece). Each mark has a one-line story. |
| **Shape language** | One primary shape (circle, square, triangle) tied to temperament and echoed in hair, garment, and asset. |
| **The pose** | One stance only they strike. If a Squadmate could strike it, redesign. |

## Design process (in this order, no skipping)

1. **Thesis** — three lines: the mechanism they embody; what they want; what it costs them. Costs must be visible on the body.
2. **Shape and silhouette** — pick the primary shape. Draft twenty silhouettes in words (three words each: mass, edge, asset). Keep three.
3. **Signature asset** — name it, give it a material, decide how it changes state (lit / unlit, full / drained, whole / cracked). It must animate.
4. **Colour identity** — dominant and accent. Check the cast lock table. Decide where the accent lives (never more than 10 percent of the body).
5. **Wear history** — five marks, one line each.
6. **Asymmetry** — one deliberate asymmetry (one sleeve, one shoulder, one side of the head). Symmetric designs read as uniforms.
7. **Motif** — a small repeated form (a ring, a fold, a notch) that appears at least three times across the design at different scales.
8. **Three directions** — render three genuinely different answers to the same thesis on one sheet. Choose one. Never render one direction and call it done.
9. **Turnaround, expression, key art** — only after the direction is chosen.
10. **Drift test** — the chosen design in three rooms. Re-cut on any drift.

## Competition rubric (score 0 to 3, gate 18 of 21)

| Axis | 3 means |
|---|---|
| Originality | Not a default, not a genre stock figure, not an existing property; a stranger would ask who designed it |
| Readability | Passes silhouette and three-value at thumbnail |
| Story in the design | Wants, costs, and history are legible without text |
| Mechanism embodiment | The agentic concept is in the body, not beside it |
| Appeal | A viewer wants to see them move; the pose promises motion |
| Consistency | Front, side, back, and expressions agree |
| Craft | Line, shading, and material discipline of a key visual |

## Ren, the Operator — redesigned

**Thesis.** Embodies the single agent loop. Wants to see the whole city at once. It costs him stamina every time he summons: the more he sees, the dimmer he gets.

**Shape.** Vertical rectangle: tall, narrow, structural. Hair, coat panels, and shards all read as verticals.

**Signature asset 1, the Crown of Shards.** The Ring is not a halo. It is nine hand-sized obsidian shards orbiting at brow height, each with a cyan fracture running through it. Only the lit shards are context; unlit shards are black glass. When the ring is full, the shards touch and hum. When compaction happens, twelve shards fuse into one denser, smaller shard with a brighter fracture. The crown is asymmetric: the shards orbit on a tilted plane, higher on his left.

**Signature asset 2, the Filament Scarf.** A long scarf woven from fibre-optic thread, charcoal when unlit, that trails to the knee. Stamina is the scarf: full stamina lights it cyan from collar to tip; each summon dims it by a hand's width from the tip up. One amber thread runs its full length and never dims. That thread is the system prompt.

**Garment.** Not a trench coat. A sleeveless, floor-length charcoal split-front duster over a fitted high-collar undershirt, so the arms and bracers read. The duster has vertical quilted channels that echo the shards. Slim dark trousers, low boots with worn brass toe caps.

**Bracers.** Forearms wrapped in dark leather bracers inscribed with fine cyan script. The inscription is the coat lining moved to the body: rules he runs a thumb across before acting. The left bracer is newer than the right (replaced after a burn).

**Belt.** No floating tool slots. Four flat obsidian discs set into the belt, each a summon circle in miniature. A summon lifts one disc off the belt, it expands into the instrument, and returns.

**Wear history.**
1. Burn scars across the knuckles of both hands, from summons held too long.
2. The right bracer is cracked and stitched with cyan thread.
3. A pale streak in the hair on the left temple, where the crown orbits closest. The shards leave marks.
4. The scarf tip is frayed and permanently dark: the last hand's width never relights. He has been at the edge before.
5. A small brass key on a cord at the throat, from the first Gate he ever opened.

**Face.** Mid thirties. Long face, straight brows, a broken and reset nose. Eyes narrow and pale grey with cyan reflection from the crown. Hair short on the sides, long and swept back on top, one lock always falling across the pale streak. A calm mouth. He has never been surprised on screen.

**Motif.** The fracture line: in the shards, in the bracer stitching, in the split of the duster.

**The pose.** Weight on the back foot, left hand raised palm-down over the belt, fingers spread, head tilted so the crown's high side faces camera. A conductor's stillness before the summon.

**Three directions to render.**
- **A, Shardwright** (the design above): crown of shards, filament scarf, split duster.
- **B, Keeper**: heavy oilskin coat, the ring as a brass astrolabe strapped to the chest with nine rotating shard-arms, a cyan lantern on a chain, round shapes, older and heavier.
- **C, Courier**: sleek and fast, the ring as a circuit tattoo on a shaved left side of the head that lights per shard, a bandolier of glass vials as tool slots, triangular shapes, younger and sharper.

Recommendation: A. It is the only one where cost lives on the body (the scarf) and the crown is physically dangerous to wear.

## Mira, the Conductor — redesigned

**Thesis.** Embodies orchestration. Wants every task finished by someone else's hands. It costs her baton light per summon, and she cannot summon herself.

**Shape.** Triangle: everything on her rises to a point on one side.

**Signature asset 1, the Sail Collar.** A single rigid collar that rises on her right side like a sail, bone white, edged in cyan, taller than her head. On its inner face the task graph is drawn in light: nodes appear as she plans, and a node goes dark when its Squadmate returns. The sail is the Bridge she carries.

**Signature asset 2, the Fork Baton.** Not a wand. A long black tuning fork with a cyan core between the tines. Drawing a graph node strikes the fork; the tone is the summon. The core dims per node. A full graph leaves the fork silent.

**Garment.** A bone-white asymmetric mantle that falls long on the left and short on the right, so the sail side is open. Under it a fitted black high-neck bodysuit with thin cyan seams that trace like a wiring harness across the back: the harness lights per active Squadmate. Fitted trousers, white boots with a black heel.

**Hair.** Silver-streaked black, in a tight braided crown that echoes the Queen's crown of light without being it. One long braid falls down the sail side.

**Wear history.**
1. The sail's cyan edge is chipped at the tip from a graph that grew too wide.
2. A burn on the left palm where she once tried to summon without the fork.
3. The mantle hem is stained with graph light that never washed out.
4. A row of small notches carved into the fork's handle, one per season of service.
5. A Squadmate's sigil pinned inside the mantle: one who did not return.

**Face.** Late thirties. Sharp features, high cheekbones, one dark and one very pale grey eye. Thin brows, a small straight scar across the bridge of the nose. A closed mouth that lifts at one corner when a graph completes.

**Motif.** The triangle: sail, mantle hem, the fork's tines.

**The pose.** Fork held out horizontally at arm's length, sail catching the light, body turned so the harness lines on the back are visible, weight forward.

**Three directions to render.**
- **A, Sail** (the design above).
- **B, Cartographer**: a long mantle printed with a city map that redraws itself; baton as a brass stylus; the graph drawn on the map at her feet; rounder, warmer.
- **C, Kite**: a mantle of layered folded panels like a paper kite, geometric, the graph drawn as folds; baton as a straight edge.

Recommendation: A. The sail is a silhouette no other character in the cast can have, and the fork gives the summon a sound.

## The squad — five distinct bodies, one team

Squadmates are not hooded uniforms. They share a base (fitted dark suit, one chest sigil) and nothing else. Each is a different shape family and carries one asset tied to its role.

| Squadmate | Shape | Body | Asset | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | Small circle | Short, compact, always crouched or perched | Goggle-lantern on the forehead that projects what it sees; a cloak that is a folded map | Moss green dominant, pale cyan accent |
| **Coder** | Sharp square | Angular, long-limbed, precise | Twin forearm bracers that extrude glyph-tools; a square sigil | Deep cyan dominant, white accent |
| **Reviewer** | Heavy circle | Broad, planted, slow to move | A hand lantern that lights only on a defect; a heavy ledger strapped to the back | Amber dominant, charcoal accent |
| **Tester** | Narrow triangle | Tall, thin, fast | A quiver of dummy pins; every pin is one test case | Chalk white dominant, one crimson thread |
| **The Queen** | Wide triangle | Tallest, upright, arms often folded | A crown of light that fills as her ring fills; gold trim on the suit | Gold dominant, navy accent |

Lineup test: the five in silhouette, side by side, must read as five different shapes. If two merge, the design is wrong.

## Presentation standard (what a finished character ships as)

1. **Direction sheet** — three directions, same thesis, one image, each labelled in post.
2. **Turnaround** — four views of the chosen direction.
3. **Expression sheet** — four states tied to the mechanism (idle, summoning, cost hits, resolution).
4. **Callout sheet** — the signature assets in isolation with state changes (lit, drained, cracked).
5. **Key art** — one full-bleed frame of the character in their room striking their pose, lit per the image system.
6. **Element** — the approved turnaround saved as a reference Element; ID recorded.

A character with fewer than these six artifacts is not cast-locked.
