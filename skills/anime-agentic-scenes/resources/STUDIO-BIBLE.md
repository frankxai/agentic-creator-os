# Studio bible — foundations for premium work

This is the pre-production package. Nothing here is a prompt. It is what a director, art director, and character lead agree on before the first drawing, so that every later decision is a consequence rather than a guess. The earlier documents (visual grammar, season bible, cinema protocol, character forge) remain valid as process; this document sits above them and fixes the world, the look, and the way of working.

---

## 0. The problem we are solving

The first renders were generic because the world was generic. "Vertical megacity at night, cyan glow, man in a long coat" is the statistical centre of the genre. No amount of pipeline discipline rescues a centre-of-distribution premise. Premium work starts with a premise nobody else has, expressed in a material the camera can see.

Rule one of this bible: **the world must be made of one visible material that costs something to use.** That single decision gives us a render style no one else has, a cost metaphor that needs no meter, and a design language every character inherits.

---

## 1. World thesis — three candidates, one recommendation

| | A. INK | B. LOOM | C. KILN |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Premise** | The world is drawn in living ink on an endless paper city. Agents are ink-borne. Every action is a brushstroke that uses pigment. | The world is woven. Threads are messages, looms are servers, cloth is state. Agents are weavers; swarms are patterns. | The world is glass, and heat is compute. Context is a vessel blown from molten glass; it holds only so much before it cracks. |
| **Cost made visible** | Ink runs pale, then dry. Brush hairs split. | Spools run out. Threads fray and snap. | Furnaces dim. Glass cools and locks. |
| **Compaction** | Folding the page: many strokes pressed into one denser mark. | Felting: many threads pressed into one solid cloth. | Annealing: reheat and merge two vessels into one thicker one. |
| **Orchestration** | A master calligrapher who draws the outline; apprentices fill the strokes. | The pattern-master at the head loom; each weaver takes one band. | The gaffer at the bench; assistants each blow one part. |
| **Consensus** | Several brushes must agree on a character before it is inked over pencil. | Warp threads must all hold or the cloth will not take a pattern. | Two glass pieces must reach the same temperature to fuse. |
| **Human gate** | The seal stamp in red, the only colour the ink does not own. | The shears. Only a human cuts cloth from the loom. | Opening the furnace door. Only a human touches the fire. |
| **Render signature** | Sumi ink wash plus hard cel, paper texture visible, one red seal. Unlike anything on screen today. | Woven texture across every surface, colour in bands, moiré as glitch. | Refractive, luminous, glass caustics on cel characters. |
| **Risk** | Ink worlds exist in games; must stay modern (brutalist, streetwear, Swiss type) not period. | Hardest to render legibly; thread detail collapses at distance. | Beautiful but drifts toward photoreal; hardest to keep cel. |
| **Uniqueness** | High | High | Medium |
| **Teachability** | Highest: every engineer knows "the page fills up" | High | Medium |

**Recommendation: A, INK, with the modern clause.** The paper is not scrolls; it is brutalist concrete rendered as paper white. The ink is not calligraphy; it is graffiti, technical drawing, and circuit trace. The characters wear contemporary silhouettes, not robes. The single red is a seal, a spray-can cap, a wax stamp. Ink gives the series a render identity in one frame, a cost model with no meters, and a compaction metaphor (folding) that is more precise than the ring ever was.

Working title under INK: **PAGE**. Season one: *The First Stroke*.

Everything below assumes INK. The other two are kept as documented alternatives in case A fails the first look test.

## 2. Style DNA (the look, fixed)

Three pillars. Every visual decision must be defensible from one of them.

| Pillar | What it means on screen | What it forbids |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink on paper** | Backgrounds are ink wash on visible paper grain: pooled edges, dry-brush texture, deliberate bleed. Characters are hard cel with a single inked outline that varies in weight like a real brush. | Airbrushed gradients, glossy surfaces, neon bloom, chromatic aberration, volumetric god rays. |
| **Brutalist modern** | Architecture is monolithic concrete rendered as paper white, sharp shadow, massive negative space. Type on surfaces is Swiss, sentence case, never decorative. Clothing is contemporary: technical outerwear, workwear, streetwear cut sharp. | Period costume, fantasy armour, ornament, filigree, glowing runes, kimono-as-default. |
| **One red** | The palette is paper white, three greys, one ink black, one ink blue-black, and a single red. Red appears only for the human gate, failure, and the Traitor. Nothing else is ever red. | Cyan-and-amber sci-fi, rainbow sigils, more than one accent hue on screen. |

### Palette (locked)

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| paper | #F3EFE6 | Backgrounds, architecture, negative space |
| paper-shadow | #D9D3C6 | Shadow side of concrete, wash mid tone |
| ink-wash | #6B6E78 | Diluted ink, distance, atmosphere |
| ink | #15161A | Line, deepest wash, hair |
| ink-blue | #1E2A44 | Living ink, the active medium, agents in motion |
| seal-red | #B3261E | The Stamp, failure, the Traitor. Nothing else |

The world is light, not dark. This alone separates it from ninety percent of "AI anime". Night scenes are ink-heavy paper, never black.

### Line and shading
- One outline weight per distance band: heavy near, medium mid, hairline far.
- Two-tone cel on characters, no third tone, no gradient.
- Backgrounds carry no outline; they are wash and edge only.
- Grain: paper fibre at 2 percent, film grain none. The texture is the paper, not the lens.

### Camera
- Two lenses only: 24mm for space, 85mm for faces and hands.
- Every wide shot has at least 40 percent empty paper.
- Aspect 2.39:1 letterboxed inside 16:9; vertical cuts recompose, never crop.

## 3. The world of PAGE

**The City** is a single sheet of paper the size of a country, folded into districts. Buildings are ink drawn on it, some finished, some still pencil. The horizon is the edge of the page. Beyond it is unwritten.

**Ink** is the medium of computation. Anything living is ink. An agent is an ink figure that can hold a brush. When an agent acts, it draws. What it draws becomes real on the page. Drawing uses the agent's own ink; the figure goes pale as it works.

**The Well** replaces the Vault: an inkwell sunk into the page where finished strokes are stored. Reading from the Well means dipping the brush, which costs a walk to the well and a little pigment.

**The Fold** replaces compaction: when a district's page is full, the master folds it; a hundred strokes become one dense crease that still holds the shape but not the detail.

**Margins** replace the Rim and the Gates: every district has a margin, and in the margin are stamps from other pages. Pressing a stamp pulls a tool from another sheet. The stamp's ink is not yours; it costs the other page.

**The Drafting Table** replaces the Bridge: the conductor works on a raised table above the district, drawing the outline of a task in pencil. Apprentices ink the lines. The conductor never inks.

**The Seal** stays, in red, the only red. The Founder's stamp, pressed by a hand in a plain white cuff, is the only thing the ink cannot draw.

**Wards** are hatch marks across a doorway. Crossing them smudges the ink; a smudge is a hook firing.

**The Practice Sheet** replaces the Dojo: a page of pre-drawn targets. Strike them and they turn solid ink or stay pencil.

**The Round Table** replaces the Council: seven brushes, one sheet; a line becomes real only when a majority of brushes have traced it.

The visual grammar rows carry over one to one. Only the material changed. `visual-grammar.md` is to be re-keyed to INK before production; the concept-to-cost mapping is unchanged.

## 4. Cast under INK (briefs, forge process applies)

Every character is ink, so every character has an **ink level** visible on their body, a **brush** that is their signature asset, and a **shape** from the forge. The forge's seven tests apply. These are the briefs; the forge produces the designs.

| Cast | One line | Silhouette | Brush | Where their ink shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ren, the Operator** | A solo draughtsman who draws faster than he can refill. | Tall vertical; oversized technical parka open over a slim base, one sleeve pushed up. | A single long round brush carried across the back like a pole. Hairs split as he tires. | His right forearm and hand, inked solid at full, fading to pencil outline as he spends. |
| **Mira, the Conductor** | She draws in pencil what others must ink. | Triangle; a rigid drafting apron that stands off the body on one side like a board. | A carpenter's pencil, flat, behind the ear. She never holds a brush. | Her outline itself: pencil-grey when planning, inked only when the graph completes. |
| **Scout** | Sees the page before it is drawn. | Small circle, crouched, hooded. | A fine liner, zero-point. | The hood's interior, a wash that shows what she has seen. |
| **Coder** | Draws structure, never scenery. | Sharp square; a straight-edge harness across the chest. | Twin ruling pens. | The harness edges, solid to pencil. |
| **Reviewer** | Finds the stroke that is wrong. | Heavy circle; a broad apron, sleeves rolled. | A wide flat brush loaded with wash, used only to reveal. | The apron front, stained. |
| **Tester** | Strikes every target on the practice sheet. | Narrow triangle; a quiver of bamboo pens. | The pens. Each is one case. | The quiver, pens go pale as they are used. |
| **The Queen** | The head loom of a phalanx, first to run dry. | Wide triangle; a broad drafting collar. | A brush the size of a broom. | Her whole outline, the first to go pencil. |
| **The Traitor** | Draws two lines where one was asked. | Mirrors its host. | A brush with a split tip. | A red bleed in the ink, always at the wrist. |
| **The Founder** | A hand and a plain white cuff, and the seal. | A hand. | None. The seal is not a brush. | Never. The Founder is not ink. |

Design rule from the forge, restated for INK: **one signature asset, one shape, one place the cost shows.** No wear-mark lists on the body; the material carries the history.

## 5. Signature devices (what makes the series recognisable in one shot)

1. **The visible brush.** Every summon is drawn on screen by a brush we can see, in real time, in the character's own line. No effects. Sound is brush on paper.
2. **The pale.** Cost is never a meter. It is the figure going pale from the extremities inward. The audience learns to read it in episode one and never needs it explained again.
3. **The held frame.** Every Cost beat holds a still frame for exactly three seconds with no sound. The series' rhythm is built on this silence.
4. **The fold.** Compaction is a physical fold of the frame itself, with the paper crease visible in every later shot of that district. Consequences persist in the image.
5. **Red once per episode.** The seal, or a failure, or the Traitor. If red appears twice, the episode is wrong.
6. **Title in the margin.** The episode title is written by a brush in the paper margin in the last shot, sentence case, Swiss type drawn by hand.
7. **The unwritten edge.** Every episode ends on the page edge, the horizon of blank paper. What the viewer builds next is off-page.

## 6. Production pipeline (how the best would do it with these tools)

The pipeline has eyes at every gate. No stage advances without a human or a vision-capable session looking at the output. A session that cannot see images does research, writing, and prep only; it never spends credits on frames.

| Stage | Owner | Input | Output | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Reference boards** | Art director | Style DNA | Three boards: material (ink on paper, real photographs of wash and brush), architecture (brutalist concrete, real), fashion (technical outerwear, real). Categories, not franchises. | AD approves boards before any generation |
| **2. Look test** | AD + director | Boards | Six frames of the same empty district in the INK style across candidate lanes. Score with eyes. Lock one lane. | Director locks the lane; recorded in `keyframes.yaml` |
| **3. Silhouette pass** | Character lead | Forge brief | A sheet of sixteen solid black silhouettes per character. No detail. Pick one. | Silhouette test at 64 px |
| **4. Sketch ladder** | Character lead | Chosen silhouette as image reference | Image-to-image, one change per pass, six to ten passes, each compared to the previous. Never restart from text. | Forge rubric, 18 of 21 |
| **5. Turnaround, expression, callout** | Character lead | Approved sketch as reference | The three sheets, same lane, same reference. | Consistency check across sheets |
| **6. Element or identity lock** | Pipeline | Approved turnaround | Reference Element (multi-cast scenes) and, for Ren and Mira, a trained identity if the lane supports it. | Drift test in three districts |
| **7. Location plates** | Background lead | Boards, look test | One empty plate per district, ink wash, 2k. | AD approval; saved as Elements |
| **8. Keyframes** | Director | Plates plus cast Elements plus pre-viz | Four candidates per beat, three-lens critique, one refine, 4k upscale. | Cinema rubric, 24 of 30 |
| **9. Motion** | Director | Gated still plus cast reference | One move, five seconds, no audio from the model. | Motion doctrine |
| **10. Composite** | Compositor | Frames, motion, titles | Letterbox, paper grain, margin title drawn by hand, fold creases carried across shots. | Director sign-off |
| **11. Sound** | Sound | Cut | Brush-on-paper library, the three-second silence, one music bed. | Director sign-off |
| **12. Release** | Producer | Cut | 90 second pilot, key art, three vertical cuts. | Virality score before publish |

Every stage writes its receipts (job ids, scores, who approved) into `keyframes.yaml`. A frame without a receipt did not happen.

### Roles mapped to agents and humans

| Role | Who | Human gate |
|---|---|---|
| Director | Frank, with the Anime Scene Director agent preparing options | Every lock |
| Art director | A vision-capable Claude session running the three-lens critique | Board approval, lane lock |
| Character lead | Vision-capable session running the forge | Direction pick |
| Background lead | Vision-capable session | Plate approval |
| Compositor | Local tooling (After Effects, Blender grease pencil, or Remotion) | Final cut |
| Sound | `/create-music` for the bed; foley library assembled once | Final cut |
| Producer | This session, or any text-only session: budgets, receipts, PR, scheduling | Release |

## 7. Critique protocol (with eyes)

Three lenses, scored independently, reconciled by the director. One line per axis, score first, fix second, no praise.

- **Cinematographer**: composition, light, depth, lens discipline, negative space.
- **Art director**: style DNA adherence (ink, brutalist, one red), cast fidelity, taste.
- **Systems engineer**: does the mechanism read with sound off; is the cost visible as the pale; would an engineer sign the frame.

Hard fails regardless of score: any second red, any gradient shading on a character, any dark background, any franchise resemblance, any text the brush did not draw.

Boards are built from real photographs and real art in categories, never from existing anime, games, or studios. Each board is twelve images, one paragraph of why, and three words the AD will use in prompts.

| Board | Categories to search | Three words |

| Motion | Ink drop high-speed footage, brush calligraphy filmed overhead, paper folding | stroke, pool, crease |

## 9. Competition and festival posture

What juries reward, in order: originality of premise, craft consistency, story legible without dialogue, sound design, restraint. What they punish: genre default, effects over drawing, more than one idea per frame, AI tells (gradient skin, glow, extra fingers, franchise echo).

The pilot is built for that list. Ninety seconds, no dialogue, one mechanism (the first stroke), one red, one fold, one held silence, one title drawn in the margin. If the pilot needs a voiceover to be understood, the design failed.

Targets: animation and motion festivals with an experimental or short category, design competitions with a motion or identity category, and the AI film festivals that judge craft rather than novelty. Submission package: pilot, key art, a one-page process sheet showing the silhouette ladder (juries reward visible process).
