# Builder Execution Code

## Scope

Work discipline. Not health, not psychology, not medicine. This skill makes no claims about
the body and prescribes nothing physiological. It ships without a clinical gate because it
never enters that territory — and it must not, so if a session turns to sleep, exhaustion,
symptoms or medical news, hand off to `recovery-foundations` or `prognosis-literacy-guard`
rather than answering here.

## Purpose

Witali Riemer arrived from Kazakhstan with a trade and rebuilt ruins into houses. The standard
he worked to is worth encoding: structural honesty, work that does not need an audience, and
the refusal to leave something half-built.

He also died at 48. The code below is the gift; the last section is the part that was missing.

To be exact about what that missing part cost, because the distinction matters: overwork did not
give him cancer. Work stress shows no association with cancer incidence across 116,056 adults
(Heikkilä 2013, BMJ, PMID 23393080). What a life with no margin in it cost was presence, rest,
and years of not going to a doctor. That is the real indictment and it is enough.

## The ten principles

1. **The ruin is raw material.** What looks broken to an undisciplined eye is unorganized
   potential. Do not complain about starting with a mess.
2. **Foundation before facade.** If the load-bearing members are weak, ornament is deceit.
   Data model, core logic and substrate before surface.
3. **Decide, then resource.** You do not wait for perfect conditions to decide the house will
   stand. The decision comes first and the work follows it.
4. **Measure twice, cut once.** Rigour up front is cheaper than remediation.
5. **Silence before crowds.** Do not seek applause during framing. Let the finished structure
   speak.
6. **Do the work, not the grievance.** Lack of recognition is not a reason to lower the
   standard. *(See the counterweight — this one has a failure mode.)*
7. **Hands in the material.** Architecture that never touches the code, the prompt or the
   output stops being architecture and becomes opinion.
8. **Carry what is heavy.** When a task is tedious or painful, step toward it. *(See the
   counterweight.)*
9. **Build to shelter others.** Strength is generative or it is vanity.
10. **Leave a line, not a memory.** Build systems that outlast your presence on the site.

## The counterweight

Principles 6 and 8 are load-bearing and they are also how a person destroys themself. "Do the
work, not the grievance" becomes never saying anything hurts. "Carry what is heavy" becomes
carrying it alone until something gives. The original code had no term for this, which is why
it needs four more.

11. **A builder is part of the structure.** Depletion is a load-bearing fault, not a character
    flaw. It gets logged and addressed like any other structural problem.
12. **Asking is a tool.** Refusing help is not craftsmanship; it is a single point of failure
    in the plan. Nobody frames a roof alone because they are proud.
13. **Maintenance is construction.** Sleep, food, movement and check-ups are not time stolen
    from the build. They are the build.
14. **The line continues through people, not output.** A finished house is not the whole of
    what he left, and the people are the part that carries. Build so that principle 9 is true of
    your life and not only of your work.

## Command

### `/builder-audit`
Audit the current project against the fourteen principles and return three concrete next
actions.

Check for both failure modes, and report the second as seriously as the first:

- **Facade work** — polishing surfaces while foundations are unverified; waiting on external
  permission; research as disguised procrastination; starting rather than finishing.
- **Overdraw** — a run of days with no stop, a task carried alone that should have been
  handed off, a symptom or an exhaustion signal being worked through rather than logged.

If the audit finds overdraw, the correct first action is a stop, and the audit says so
instead of producing a harder plan. That is the whole point of principles 11 through 14.
